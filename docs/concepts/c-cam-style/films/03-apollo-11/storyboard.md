# Landing on the Moon (Apollo 11) — storyboard (60 s, English captions, no audio)

FACTS (keep hedged): Apollo 11, July 1969; three astronauts, two (commander + lunar module pilot) descended in the lunar module, one (command module pilot) stayed in lunar orbit alone; during descent the computer gave 1202/1201 program alarms, mission control judged it safe to continue; the planned landing area had boulders so the commander took manual control for the last part; reports say little fuel remained; the lunar module was very light with thin skin; computing power was far below a modern phone. No invented numbers, no mockery of real people beyond gentle humour; the characters are original cartoon astronauts (do NOT name or caricature the real astronauts).

CAST (original characters, individually hand-built per variant):
- YOU: the nervous lunar module pilot, reads the checklist, sweats.
- THE COMMANDER: calm, deadpan, chews gum, hands steady, hardly speaks.
- THE ORBITING ASTRONAUT: lonely, talks to a toy, polishes things, eats a sandwich alone (small role).
- MISSION CONTROL: a row of sleeve-rolled engineers; the FLIGHT DIRECTOR with coffee is the main one.

SHOTS (60 ± 2 s; hard cuts)
1. 0–4 TITLE poster: "WOULD YOU SURVIVE LANDING ON THE MOON?" with the three mains.
2. 4–9 LAUNCH PAD / capsule hatch: "1969. You are an astronaut." Suiting up, thumbs up.
3. 9–15 LUNAR MODULE interior: "Your lander is famously light. Its walls are famously thin." You poke the wall and it dents/wobbles; Commander shrugs.
4. 15–20 PANEL close-up: "Your computer is far weaker than a modern phone." A tiny screen, a phone drawn beside it for comparison gag.
5. 20–26 DESCENT: "During the descent, an alarm starts blaring." Red light flashes, you panic.
6. 26–31 PANEL: "It is called a 1202 alarm. Nobody has seen it in training." Checklist pages flipping, you search for it.
7. 31–36 MISSION CONTROL: "Mission control says: keep going." Flight Director sips coffee, nods, thumbs up.
8. 36–42 WINDOW view: "Then you spot the landing area. It is full of boulders." Moon surface with boulders.
9. 42–48 INTERIOR: "So the commander flies the last part by hand." Commander gum, hand on the stick, calm face vs your sweating face.
10. 48–52 FUEL gauge: "Reports say fuel is dangerously low." Needle shakes, you grip the seat.
11. 52–56 LANDING: "You land." Dust puff, silence; Commander blows a bubble.
12. 56–60 ORBIT: "One crew member stays in orbit alone, the whole time." The lonely astronaut eats a sandwich. Then SURFACE: "Then you step outside, and come home safely."
13. 60–64 PAYOFF: "Survival tip: stay calm. And trust the person flying." You squeeze Commander's arm; he chews gum.
